GRF17308423

2023/2024 | Metal transport in Ore Fluids by Organic Matter | FT-MS and IRMPD Spectroscopy of the Structure of Gold- and Copper-Biomolecule Clusters.

This newly funded research project aims to investigates Au(III) and Cu(I) chloride complexes with organic and organosulfur ligands using FT-MS IRMPD techniques to better understand their role in gold and copper transport in aqueous fluids and ore deposit formation. The second theme of this project examines the same metal (Au and Cu) but complexed with amino acids and dipeptides to explore metal-biomolecule interactions, oligomerization, and their role in prebiotic evolution and peptide complexation. Both themes employ statse-of-the-art FT ion electrospray mass spectrometry and IRMPD spectroscopy, filling in some knowledge gaps in metal-ligand interactions and their impact on geochemical processes and prebiotic chemistry.

Method synopsis: This work will involve experimental IRMPD studies of metal-complexes with various (thio)organic and biomolecular ligands using high-resolution FT ion resonance mass spectrometry and structure-sensitive infrared multiphoton dissociation (IRMPD) spectroscopy; these experiments will be conducted using a custom-built Nd:YAG-pumped IR OPO/OPA laser beam line at HKU with ion mass-isolation capability and tunable infrared radiation from 4500 cm-1 to 2200 cm-1. Typical FT-MS IRMPD experiments will involve electrospraying dilute (mM) aqueous solutions of metal-ligands salts , followed by mass-isolation, IR laser irradiation, and IR spectral analysis. These type of experiments will be complimented by ab initio calculations , using either molecular dynamics or particle swarm optimization tools, DFT reoptimizations, and frequency calculations. Lower energy structures will then be reoptimized at higher levels of theory, for example at the MP2 and CCSD(T) level of theory, with larger basis sets and dispersion corrections. Lastly, we are quite fortunate to perform all these type of advanced theoretical calculations (G16, C4 and CP2K) on HKU's HPC2021 network. Thanks HKU! .
