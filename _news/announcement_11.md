<b>:loudspeaker: New FO-CRDS spectrometer for prebiotic studies, Nov.19, 2026 </b> :bulb: :gem: :droplet: 

<div style="text-align: justify;">
<p>
A new fibre-optic cavity ring-down spectrometer (FO-CRDS) is now up and running in our laboratory. A 460 nm laser diode has been successfully pumped into a fibre-optic cavity roughly 1.8 m long. Cavity ring-down measures absorption from the decay time of light trapped in an optical cavity, here a fibre-optic cavity, giving very high sensitivity to weak optical absorptions and to small sample volumes. The instrument combines a pulsed diode laser source, the fibre cavity and fast photodetection, and complements our existing high-PT UV–vis cells and OPO/OPA IR laser work.
</p>
<p>
This new capability will significantly advance our experimental investigations of aqueous and hydrothermal fluids and biomolecule and metal-complex chemistry. The system is currently being mated to a diamond anvil cell (DAC), whose diamond windows are transparent at 460 nm, to identify and monitor prebiotic systems at gigapascal pressures. Because FO-CRDS works with the very small sample volumes of a DAC, it should allow us to follow in situ reactions whose rates and equilibria are strongly pressure-dependent, such as condensation reactions with large negative reaction volumes, and to test which prebiotic molecules may persist in the deep, water-rich interiors of super-Earths.
</p>
