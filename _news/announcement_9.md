<b>:loudspeaker: Quartz Solubility and Silica Speciation in Hydrothermal Vapor: Experimental and DFT Insights 💎♨️⚗️🫧



<div style="text-align: justify;">
<p>
Using ab initio calculations and density functional theory (B3LYP-D3), combined with MP2 methods, we have quantified silica speciation and quartz solubility in hydrothermal water vapor from 400 to 800 °C and 50–270 bar, supported by novel U-tube flow-through reactor experiments. These quantum chemical results reveal stable gaseous silica structures as Si(OH)₄(g) and its hydrated forms Si(OH)₄·(H₂O)₂(g), Si(OH)₄·(H₂O)₄(g), and Si(OH)₄·(H₂O)₇(g), with multiple isomers identified where bonding motifs evolve from silanol-water interactions at low hydration to intricate water-water hydrogen bond networks at higher levels. Solubility constants (log K) show increasing values for less hydrated species with temperature, while higher hydrates become less stable, reflecting low silica concentrations (0.11–4.56 mmol/kg) that rise with temperature and pressure due to enhanced cluster formation in denser vapor.
</p>
<p>
These findings have important implications for silica transport in natural systems: hydrated silica species predominate in denser vapor phases but diminish at higher temperatures, facilitating quartz dissolution primarily through lower-order clusters that enhance mobility in low-pressure environments. This speciation-driven behavior is expected to influence mineral deposition, fluid-mineral interactions, and geochemical cycling in volcanic fumaroles, porphyry ore systems, and supercritical geothermal reservoirs.
</p>

</div>
