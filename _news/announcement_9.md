<b>:loudspeaker: Silica solubility and molecular speciation in hydrothermal water vapor, Oct. 22, 2025 </b> :thermometer: :droplet: :alembic:

<div style="text-align: justify;">
<p>
Together with Andri Stefánsson and colleagues, we report quartz solubility experiments at 400–800 °C and 50–270 bar using a U-tube flow-through reactor, combined with DFT (B3LYP-D3) and MP2 calculations of gaseous silica speciation. Measured silica concentrations in water vapor remain low (0.11–4.56 mmol/kg) but increase with both temperature and pressure. Quartz dissolution into vapor is described by the equilibrium
</p>
<p style="text-align:center; color:#000; background:none;">
SiO₂(s) + (n + 2)H₂O(g) ⇌ Si(OH)₄·(H₂O)<sub>n</sub>(g)
</p>
<p>
with the dominant gaseous species Si(OH)₄(g), Si(OH)₄·(H₂O)₂(g), Si(OH)₄·(H₂O)₄(g), and Si(OH)₄·(H₂O)₇(g) (n = 0, 2, 4, 7). Isomer complexity grows with hydration as bonding motifs shift from silanol–water contacts to water–water hydrogen-bond networks. Equilibrium constants for the lower hydrates increase with temperature, whereas higher hydrates become less favorable; derived enthalpies are nearly temperature-independent, while entropy and heat capacity decrease with hydration number. These results constrain silica transport in volcanic fumaroles, porphyry systems, and supercritical geothermal reservoirs. 📝 <b>Stefánsson et al., Geochim. Cosmochim. Acta 406, 44–56 (2025)</b> — <a href="https://doi.org/10.1016/j.gca.2024.10.022">https://doi.org/10.1016/j.gca.2024.10.022</a>
</p>
</div>
