<b>:loudspeaker: Advances in Hydrothermal Geochemistry: From Molecular-Scale Processes to Natural Systems, July 13, 2026 </b> (Goldschmidt 2026 Montréal, Session 04g-O1 Co-Convener with Andri Stefánsson) :droplet: :microscope: :dna: :thermometer:

<div style="text-align: justify;">
<p>
Session 04g-O1 on Advances in Hydrothermal Geochemistry was a highlight of Goldschmidt 2026 at the Palais des congrès de Montréal (Room 524B). Together with co-convener Andri Stefánsson, the afternoon brought together experimental, spectroscopic, and theoretical perspectives spanning molecular-scale processes through to natural hydrothermal systems, including invited talks on kinetic barriers and metastable equilibria, uranyl chloride speciation, and fluids beyond the critical point.
</p>
<p>
<b>🗣 GS2026 talk 37357 (Lemke & Clarke):</b> Our high-PT UV-vis study examined the kinetic stability of unnatural Hachimoji-type nucleosides and nucleobases under simulated prebiotic hydrothermal conditions. Using a custom-built spectroscopic cell with an Au-electroplated interior and sapphire windows, we measured time-dependent spectra of isoguanosine, isocytidine, isoguanine, and isocytosine at 90–180 °C and 7.5 MPa. Global spectral-kinetic fitting of the stopped-flow datasets yielded first-order rate constants; as a benchmark, isoguanosine at pH 6.5 and 120 °C decomposes with k = 9.4 × 10<sup>−6</sup> s<sup>−1</sup> (t<sub>1/2</sub> = 20.5 h), moderately faster than canonical guanosine under the same conditions. These results provide the first quantitative hydrothermal stability data for an expanded eight-letter genetic alphabet and constrain why nature may have selected the canonical nucleosides.
</p>
<p>
