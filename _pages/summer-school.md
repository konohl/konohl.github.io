**Molecules Before Life — the physical chemistry of prebiotic worlds (2027)**

Most students first meet the origin of life as a story. The scientific problem is different: before cells existed, simple molecules in water had to assemble long enough to react and organise under heat, shifting pH, and mineral surfaces. Hot water can build complex chemistry and also destroy it. This programme asks which conditions could have let prebiotic molecules last long enough to matter.

In 2027 the Department of Earth and Planetary Sciences at HKU will run an off-school laboratory programme for about 32 HKAGE senior secondary students. Participants will treat early-Earth chemistry as something that can be measured in a safe teaching laboratory — temperature, pH, minerals, and spectroscopy — work in small teams, design an investigation, and defend what the data show and where they are unclear.

### Approach

The programme will proceed in stages: early-Earth conditions (including views of a warm, possibly acidic ocean and hydrothermal gradients); the stability of simple organics (sugars and nucleic-acid components) under heat, pH, and mineral surfaces; mentored mini-projects; then posters or short talks and a brief written reflection. Teaching will be in English. Junior-secondary science is sufficient; HKDSE Chemistry is not required.

### Goals

Students should leave able to explain how water, energy, temperature, pH, and mineral surfaces likely constrained chemistry before life; distinguish a popular origin-of-life narrative from a kinetic and stability argument; use safe laboratory methods and basic rate / half-life ideas; and report team results with clear, honest uncertainty — including messy or hard-to-interpret experiments.

### Context

Teaching is grounded in research on how heat, pH, pressure, and mineral surfaces affect whether simple organics persist or break down in aqueous and hydrothermal settings — physical chemistry relevant to the early Earth before biology.

Delivery: May–September 2027, subject to Gifted Education Fund approval.
