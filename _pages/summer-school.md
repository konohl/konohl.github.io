## Summer school 2027

**Molecules Before Life — the physical chemistry of prebiotic worlds**

Young students usually meet the origin of life as a story rather than as a hands-on experience. The real problem is harder — and more interesting. Before there were cells on the early Earth, simple molecules in solution (most probably the ocean) had to assemble long enough to react and organise, most likely under relatively hot conditions, shifting pH, and mineral surfaces. We now know that hot water can help build rather complex chemistry — and also erase it. The question this summer school tries to address is which conditions could have let prebiotic molecules last long enough to matter.

In 2027 the Department of Earth and Planetary Sciences at HKU will run an off-school laboratory programme for about 32 HKAGE senior secondary students. Instead of a classroom rehearsal of a narrative of life’s beginnings, students will treat early-Earth chemistry as something you can measure and observe in the lab: temperature, pH, minerals, and various forms of spectroscopy, in a safe teaching laboratory. Students will work in small teams, design an investigation, and later defend what their data showed — and where the data made little sense, or where understanding the data was hard.

### Approach

The programme will move through several stages. Students will begin with conditions generally thought to have prevailed on the early Earth — including accepted views of an early warm, possibly acidic ocean, with liquid water present and hydrothermal gradients in pressure and temperature. They will then look at how simple organic molecules (sugars, RNA and DNA fragments and their components) hold up — or fail — under high temperature, heat, pH, and mineral surfaces. Students will then run mentored mini-projects and finish with posters or short talks and a short written reflection on what they learned. English will be the language of teaching. Junior-secondary science is enough; HKDSE Chemistry is not required.

### Goals

By the end of the summer school, students should be able to explain why water, energy in its different forms, temperature, pH, and mineral surfaces most probably set limits on chemistry before life got started. They should be able to tell a popular origin-of-life story apart from a kinetic and stability argument; use safe laboratory methods; navigate basic rates, chemical kinetics and half-life ideas; and report a team experiment with uncertainty stated clearly, comprehensively, and with academic honesty — including runs that were messy or hard to understand.

### Context

The science behind the teaching comes from research on how extreme conditions — heat, pH, pressure, and different mineral surfaces — most probably decided whether simple organic molecules persisted or broke down in water and hydrothermal settings: the physical chemistry of molecules that could have existed on the early Earth, long before biology came to be.

Delivery is planned for May–September 2027, subject to Gifted Education Fund approval of the proposed programme.
