**Molecules Before Life — physical chemistry of prebiotic worlds (2027)**

Students will meet the origin of life as a story. The scientific problem is different. Before cells existed, simple molecules in water had to persist, react and organise under heat, pH and mineral surfaces. Hot water can build molecules and destroy them. The open question is which conditions let prebiotic molecules last long enough to matter.

This off-school programme, run by the Department of Earth and Planetary Sciences at HKU for HKAGE members, will treat that question as a laboratory problem rather than a narrative. About 32 senior secondary students will work in safe teaching laboratories with heating, pH, minerals and simple UV–Vis spectroscopy. They will design a small team investigation and defend what the data do — and do not — show.

### Who

About 32 HKAGE senior secondary students; Department of Earth and Planetary Sciences, HKU. The medium of instruction will be English. No HKDSE Chemistry will be required: junior-secondary science and basic algebra will be enough.

### Approach

Phase by phase, students will move from early Earth and liquid water, through the stability of simple organics under heat, pH and mineral surfaces, into mentored mini-projects, and finish with posters or short talks and honest reflection.

### Goals

Students will explain why water, energy, temperature, pH and mineral surfaces constrain chemistry before life; separate a popular origin-of-life story from a kinetic and stability argument; use safe lab methods and basic rate / half-life ideas; design, run and report a team mini-project with uncertainty stated clearly; and persist when an experiment is messy.

### Deliverables

Each team will present a poster or short talk. Each student will write an individual reflection. The programme will also leave a reusable teaching dataset pack for later cohorts.

### Context

The scientific core comes from research on how heat, pH and mineral surfaces control whether simple organics persist or break down in aqueous and hydrothermal settings — the physical chemistry of molecules that could have existed on an early Earth, long before biology.

Delivery is planned for May–September 2027, subject to Gifted Education Fund approval of the proposed programme.
