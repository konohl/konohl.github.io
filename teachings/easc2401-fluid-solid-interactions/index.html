Fluid/Solid Interactions

Undergraduate course on the physical and chemical principles that govern Earth processes at fluid–solid interfaces. Topics include equilibrium thermodynamics, phase diagrams (melting, crystallization, condensation), energy transfer, reaction kinetics and isotope fractionation, and the forces that drive fluid flow and particle transport. It is a core requirement for Earth System Science and Geology majors.

Number: EASC2401

Instructor: Kono H. Lemke

Term: Second semester

Course Overview

This course provides an overview of the physical and chemical processes that govern fluid–solid interactions in Earth environments, including mineral solubility in aqueous and hydrothermal fluids, reactions at mineral–water and related interfaces, melt–crystal equilibria, and fluid flow and particle transport at fluid–solid and melt–solid interfaces.

Students will:

  • Understand basic principles of equilibrium thermodynamics as applied to the Earth Sciences
  • Use phase diagrams to explain processes of fluid/solid interactions, in particular systems containing melts and solids
  • Describe how energy is exchanged throughout the Earth System
  • Demonstrate an understanding of principles governing isotope exchange reactions in single-phase systems and across fluid/solid and fluid/gas interfaces
  • Understand fluid flow and particle transport at fluid–solid and melt–solid interfaces

Prerequisites

  • Pass in EASC1401 or EASC1402

Textbooks

  • Practical Chemical Thermodynamics for Geoscientists, Bruce Fegley (2012), Academic Press
  • Earth Materials, Kevin Hefferan and John O’Brien (2010), John Wiley & Sons

Grading

  • Midterm examination: 15%
  • Projects 1–3 (oral examination): 15% each
  • Final examination (written and oral): 40%

Each student first sits a written paper and is then examined orally, individually, on the material of the examination.
